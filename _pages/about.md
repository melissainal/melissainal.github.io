I am a cellular biologist and neuroscientist dedicated to dissecting the fundamental cell biological mechanisms that govern neural circuit development and synaptic architecture. By combining advanced optical microscopy—particularly in vivo single-molecule localization microscopy (SMLM/STORM)—with genetic model systems, tissue clearing, and quantitative image analysis, my research investigates how motoneurons and target muscle cells coordinate morphological recognition and functional synapse formation.

I earned my Ph.D. in Cellular Biology from the University of Georgia (2025) in the laboratory of [Dr. Daichi Kamiyama](https://research.franklin.uga.edu/kamiyama/). My doctoral dissertation (*"High-Resolution Characterization of Filopodial Interactions Guiding Neuromuscular Circuit Development in Drosophila"*) pioneered a first-of-its-kind in vivo super-resolution imaging pipeline in motor neurons, achieving 20-nm lateral resolution. This work uncovered a novel axon guidance mechanism mediated by widespread non-synaptic muscle filopodia (myopodia) and identified key adhesion molecules (Fas2 and Ncad) that direct partner recognition at the developing neuromuscular junction.

### Research Interests
* **Neural Circuit Assembly & Axon Guidance:** How dynamic cell-cell contacts and transient filopodial structures steer growth cones and establish synaptic specificity during neurodevelopment.
* **Super-Resolution Microscopy & Optical Pipeline Development:** Implementing single- and multi-color SMLM (STORM/PALM), structured illumination microscopy (SIM), and deep-tissue cleared imaging (CLARITY) to resolve macromolecular architectures below the diffraction limit of light.
* **Synaptogenesis & Sub-cellular Protein Topography:** Mapping the spatial distribution and nano-organization of pre- and post-synaptic scaffold proteins using split-GFP complementation and high-fidelity immunofluorescence in intact organisms.
* **Quantitative Bioimage Informatics:** Developing automated image processing workflows (ImageJ/Fiji, Python, R) to extract high-content spatial metrics from complex volumetric datasets.

### Leadership, Mentorship & Community Engagement
Beyond bench research and microscopy development, I am deeply committed to scientific community building, student mentorship, and broadening access to STEM. Throughout my doctoral training at the University of Georgia, I served as President of the Cellular Biology Graduate Student Association and Co-President of the Developmental Biology Alliance. In these roles, I represented graduate students in departmental leadership town halls, organized annual symposia and research retreats with external keynote speakers, secured departmental sponsorship for student professional development, and launched wellness and collaborative initiatives such as student "Science Talk Spaces" and data visualization workshops.

Mentorship has been a central and rewarding pillar of my academic career. I have guided and trained high school interns, undergraduate researchers, and junior graduate students in rigorous experimental design, fly genetics, and advanced optical microscopy (including STORM and confocal imaging), supporting trainees to present their findings at regional research symposia. In the broader community, I continue this service as a volunteer mentor with the Ocean Discovery Institute's College Access Program in San Diego, helping high school students navigate the college admissions process, and as a science communication volunteer at the Fleet Science Center, engaging the public with hands-on cellular and biological science.
