I believe that meaningful scientific progress is inseparable from the communities that foster it. Scientific research reaches its highest potential when paired with intentional leadership, active student advocacy, and inclusive mentorship that opens doors for future generations. Across my academic career and continuing in the broader community, I have dedicated myself to building supportive institutional environments, mentoring emerging scientists, and connecting biological research with the public. Continuing to serve—both within academic and professional settings and through community-based educational initiatives—remains one of the most fulfilling dimensions of my scientific life.

---

## Academic Leadership & Institutional Service

### President — Cellular Biology Graduate Student Association (2022 – 2023)
Serving as GSA President allowed me to act as a vital bridge between the graduate student body and departmental leadership. In this role, I facilitated constructive dialogue through departmental town halls, successfully secured departmental funding to sponsor graduate student attendance at professional development conferences (such as UGA Career Day), and organized community-wide gatherings bringing together students, faculty, and research staff. Recognizing the intense pressures of doctoral training, I launched targeted initiatives prioritizing holistic well-being and practical skills, including dedicated Student Wellness Days, write-in sessions, and data visualization workshops. This experience reinforced my deep conviction that academic environments thrive when leadership proactively cultivates empathy, resource equity, and transparent communication—values I am committed to advancing wherever my scientific journey takes me.

### Co-President — Developmental Biology Alliance Graduate Student Association (2022 – 2023)
As Co-President of the Developmental Biology Alliance, I led the planning and execution of our annual interdisciplinary research events, including the DBA Spring Retreat and Fall Symposium. A primary focus of my tenure was curating a high-caliber scientific program: I coordinated the invitation, travel, and hosting of four distinguished keynote speakers from premier institutions across the nation. Beyond organizing the logistics, this role was deeply meaningful because it provided an inclusive regional stage for junior trainees and graduate peers to present their work alongside world-class developmental biologists. It affirmed for me the power of interdisciplinary alliances in breaking down academic silos and building collaborative scientific networks.

### Graduate Council Representative — Department of Cellular Biology (2021 – 2022)
As our departmental representative on the university-wide Graduate Student Council, I advocated on behalf of graduate and professional students during council sessions, addressing pressing campus issues including student healthcare access, financial stipends, and academic resource policies. Representing the collective voice of my peers in institutional discussions taught me the critical importance of governance, transparency, and systemic advocacy. It solidified my lifelong commitment to standing up for equity, fair working conditions, and supportive policies across academic and institutional spaces.

### Social Chair — Developmental Biology Alliance & Cellular Biology GSA (2020 – 2022)
During periods often marked by isolation in graduate school, I served as Social Chair for both the DBA and CB-GSA with the explicit goal of fostering genuine belonging and camaraderie. I initiated the "Science Talk Space"—an informal, low-pressure forum where graduate students could share preliminary data, practice conference talks, and exchange constructive feedback before presenting at formal seminars. I also organized cohort-wide welcome events, holiday gatherings, and cross-lab mixers. Cultivating these casual, trusting spaces showed me that vibrant, collegial social networks directly bolster scientific creativity and resilience, proving that community-building is an essential ingredient of rigorous research.

## Community Mentorship & Public Outreach

### College Access Program Mentor — Ocean Discovery Institute (San Diego, CA | Aug 2026 – Present)
Working as a volunteer mentor with the Ocean Discovery Institute's College Access Program has been one of the most rewarding ways to translate my academic experience into direct community impact. I work one-on-one with high school students from historically underrepresented backgrounds in San Diego, guiding them through the complex college admissions landscape. From exploring college match lists and financial aid options to helping students find and articulate their authentic voices in personal statements, this mentorship is dedicated to demystifying higher education. For me, expanding access to STEM is not an abstract concept; it is a personal responsibility to ensure that talented, ambitious young individuals see a clear and achievable path for themselves in college and scientific careers.

### Science Communication Volunteer — Fleet Science Center (San Diego, CA | 2026)
Volunteering with the Fleet Science Center enables me to share the wonders of biology and microscopy with the broader public. I engage children, families, and visitors through interactive, hands-on scientific demonstrations, translating complex cellular concepts into accessible, curiosity-sparking conversations. This service continually reminds me that science belongs to everyone. Bridging the gap between specialized laboratory research and public curiosity is essential for fostering scientific literacy, combating misinformation, and inspiring the next generation of curious thinkers.

### Research Mentor — Kamiyama Laboratory, University of Georgia (2021 – 2022)
Within the research laboratory, I directly supervised and mentored two undergraduate researchers, four rotation graduate students, and two junior doctoral peers. I designed tailored training curricula covering *Drosophila* genetics, optical microscopy alignment, and rigorous quantitative data analysis. More importantly, I prioritized helping each mentee develop independent scientific thinking, troubleshooting resilience, and confident presentation skills. Watching trainees transition from nervous novices into researchers who can articulate their own hypotheses and present at research symposia remains one of the proudest highlights of my doctoral journey, driving my dedication to mentoring in all future scientific roles.

### Research Mentor — Nanotechnology and Biomedicine REU (2021)
Through the NSF-funded Research Experiences for Undergraduates (REU) program, I served as a primary research mentor to a visiting scholar. I provided intensive daily training in sample preparation, fluorophore optimization, and multi-color Stochastic Optical Reconstruction Microscopy (STORM). Over the course of the summer, I guided the scholar through scientific writing, statistical image quantification, and poster creation, culminating in an award-worthy presentation at a regional symposium. Mentoring in an intensive summer fellowship underscored the importance of intentional, structured guidance in accelerating an aspiring scientist's confidence and technical trajectory.

### High School Research Mentor — UGA Young Dawgs Program (2018 – 2019)
Through the Young Dawgs STEM initiative, I mentored a local high school senior, teaching delicate micro-dissection of *Drosophila* embryos and hands-on confocal microscopy. Despite the technical difficulty of the techniques, I focused on patient, step-by-step instruction that demystified advanced instrumentation for a teenage student. The mentee went on to successfully present their developmental imaging findings to an interdisciplinary cohort of peers and faculty. This early mentoring experience ignited my passion for youth outreach and taught me that high expectations combined with compassionate support can unlock remarkable potential at any educational stage.

### Youth Tutor & Volunteer — Mind Blowers & East Athens WIT (2015 – 2018)
Throughout my undergraduate years, I volunteered weekly as an after-school tutor with local Athens community organizations, providing academic homework help, literacy support, and mentorship to elementary and middle school students. Working with children in underserved neighborhoods taught me the foundational value of consistent presence, patience, and positive reinforcement. It grounded my scientific aspirations in a broader awareness of educational disparities and instilled a lasting commitment to service that continues to guide my civic life today.
