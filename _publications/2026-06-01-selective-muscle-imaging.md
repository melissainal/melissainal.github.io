### Overview & Significance
This study demonstrates that embryonic muscle cells extend clustered non-synaptic filopodia (myopodia) that actively guide motor axons toward target fields prior to synaptogenesis. These findings establish a foundation for my future research into how subcellular domains coordinate partner recognition, while offering significant translational relevance for uncovering the developmental origins of neuromuscular disorders and designing targeted re-innervation therapies.

### Abstract
Precise neuromuscular circuit assembly requires dynamic cellular interactions between motoneurons and their target muscle cells. Using selective muscle imaging and advanced optical microscopy, this study demonstrates widespread clustering of non-synaptic muscle filopodia (myopodia) across the body wall musculature during *Drosophila* embryonic development, revealing their coordinated spatial and temporal distribution during motor axon guidance and synaptogenesis.
